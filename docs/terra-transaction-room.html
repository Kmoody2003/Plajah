01

Plajah coordinates. Licensed parties perform the legal acts.

Plajah does

Listing, offers, documents, deadlines, checklists, plain-language explanations, and the audit trail.

Licensed partner does

Title search sign-off and title insurance, escrow of all money, remote online notarization, e-recording, settlement statement, 1099-S, FIRPTA.

The person does

Signs, pays, makes every decision, and submits the filings that must come from them: Property Transfer Affidavit, principal residence exemption, permits.

02

Hard rules

1

Plajah holds no money

Earnest money, closing funds and seller proceeds move between the parties and the title/escrow partner only. No smart contract, wallet or Plajah balance ever holds them. This is Plajah's own rule, and money-transmitter law points the same way.

2

Flat fees only for real estate Attorney gate

A fee tied to the price or to a closed sale risks being "acting as a broker" under MCL 339.2501. The platform's 5% direct-sales fee never applies to a property sale.

3

The recorded deed is the legal record

Michigan is a race-notice state: priority comes from recording at the county. Plajah's event chain mirrors that record and audits the process. It is never "the title."

4

Templates, not legal advice Attorney gate

Deeds and purchase agreements use attorney-approved templates filled from the parties' own answers, with a one-tap "attorney review" option. This guards against unauthorized practice of law.

Lifecycle

draft→verified→listed→offer received→under contract→contingencies→clear to close→signing→funded→recorded→post close→closed

Offers loop through counter and reject until one is accepted. Contingencies cover disclosure, inspection, financing, appraisal and title; each is cleared or waived. From any state a room can move to terminated, with a reason and a deposit instruction sent to title. Transitions are checked on the server: required documents, signatures and deadlines must be in place. The app can only request a move.

06

Data model

All transaction data is server-only: reads and writes go through /api/terra/tx/* with the service account, like Terra ingestion and Plajah ID. Files sit in Firebase Storage behind signed links, scoped by who may see them.

terraTx/{txId}
  parcelId       "detroit:<parcelNumber>"   join to terraParcels
  listingKey?    terraListings/{key}
  state          lifecycle state
  parties        { [pjId]: { role, uid, verifiedAt?, displayName } }
  terms          price, deposit, contingencies, closing date, inclusions
  deadlines      [{ kind, dueAt, satisfiedAt?, waivedAt?, waivedBy? }]
  partners       { title?, ron?, erecord? }
  head           { eventSeq, eventHash }   tip of the hash chain
  recording?     { liber, page, instrumentNo, recordedAt, county }

terraTx/{txId}/events/{seq}    append-only, hash-chained
  seq, type, actorPjId, at, payload, prevHash, hash

terraTx/{txId}/offers/{offerId}
  fromPjId, version, terms, status, expiresAt, docId

terraTx/{txId}/docs/{docId}
  kind, version, storagePath, sha256, uploadedBy,
  signatures [{ pjId, method, at, sha256AtSign }], visibility

terraTx/{txId}/tasks/{taskId}
  owner role, status, due, linked doc kind

The smart contract is this event chain

  • Every event's fingerprint includes the previous one, so any rewrite of history is detectable. Same pattern as Plajah ID history and the listing content hash.
  • A signature locks to the exact bytes signed. A new version of a document needs new signatures.
  • Later, the chain's tip can be anchored to a public chain or timestamp authority at key moments (accepted, recorded). Anchoring only: no value is ever on-chain.

08

Seller verification

Deed fraud on vacant Detroit lots is real. A listing can't go live until every step passes; a mismatch blocks it, never "warn and continue."

  1. Identity check: government ID plus a live selfie through a verification partner. Plajah keeps only the result.
  2. Name match against the owner on the parcel roll and the last vesting deed at the Register of Deeds. This covers roll lag, trusts, LLCs and estates.
  3. Entity and estate sellers upload their authority: operating agreement and resolution, probate letters of authority, or trust certification.
  4. Vacant land or an out-of-state owner adds a code mailed to the tax-bill address.

11

Permit Binder

The same room pattern for Detroit permits and applications. Plajah hosts the files and the checklist; the person submits on the government portal under their own account.

Plajah does
  • Works out which permits a project needs from zoning and the project description
  • Checks the document list and fills what it can
  • Packages a ready-to-upload set, named the way the portal expects
  • Links straight to the official form, or the portal's front page where deep links aren't supported
Plajah never
  • Submits on someone's behalf
  • Stores their portal password
  • Reads anything behind their login

A sale room links the parcel's permit binders, so open permits surface in the seller disclosure and title prep.

Beyond Detroit

Terra is Detroit-only because zoning rules are hand-encoded per city. Parcels alone (shape, owner, value) are much cheaper to add. The plan: add parcel-only counties and cities first, so search, the property passport, listings and the Transaction Room work there, then encode zoning city by city.

Michigan has no open statewide parcel layer: the state keeps one for internal use and points to each county. Each source below was checked live on 25 November 2026.

  • No open data: Wayne County outside Detroit (only a 2015 shapefile), Washtenaw, Livingston, Jackson, Berrien, Monroe, St. Clair, Bay and Marquette offer viewers or paid data only. Options: each city's or township's assessor, a records request, or a licensed statewide feed (Regrid, about $12k a year for Michigan, per end user and not sublicensable).
  • A blank licence is not permission. Get a short written data-sharing agreement from each "Ask" county before using its data commercially.
  • Owner names are personal data wherever they appear, so set display and retention rules before adding them.
  • Layer names change every year (2025, 2026 editions), so the loader finds the current layer through each county's data hub instead of hard-coding it.
  • Recording: Oakland County accepts e-recording through CSC, eRecording Partners Network, Simplifile and HopDox. Wayne County's recording page lists only in-person and mail, so confirm e-recording with the Register of Deeds before choosing a partner.

Not in version 1

  • Seller financing and land contracts. Detroit has a history of abuse, and federal and state loan-originator rules apply. Version 2, with required attorney review and a recorded memorandum.
  • Commercial property, buildings over 4 units, new construction, short sales, probate sales without letters of authority.
  • Anywhere outside Wayne County.
