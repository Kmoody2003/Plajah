**Subject:** UMONS-TAICHI in a public martial-arts heritage museum — licensing question

Dear Professor Dutoit and colleagues,

I'm writing about UMONS-TAICHI (Tits, Laraba, Caulier, Tilmanne & Dutoit, *Data
in Brief*, 2018), which we have been using under CC BY-NC-SA while building
something I hope will interest you.

We have built a digital museum of world martial arts — fifty traditions across
five continents, each with cited scholarship and a rights-cleared media layer.
It sits inside Plajah, a creator platform, in our academic section. A part of it
called the Motion Lab treats technique as data rather than illustration: a
browser player that streams real motion capture, so a visitor can watch a
technique rather than read about it. It currently plays fighting sequences from
the CMU Graphics Lab database alongside one segmented Kinect sequence from
UMONS-TAICHI, which we display as a clearly-labelled research preview.

That preview is where I need your guidance. Plajah is a commercial platform, so
the non-commercial clause means we cannot in good conscience keep your data in
the public build. I would rather ask than quietly drop it, because your dataset
is genuinely the best kinetic record of any traditional martial art in
existence, and its skill-ranking dimension — novice through expert, scored by
teachers — is something no other capture set offers. The idea that expertise
itself is legible in the data is, to me, a museum exhibit in its own right.

**What I'm asking:** whether you would permit a small number of UMONS-TAICHI
sequences (we would propose two to four, one per technique class) to be
streamed inside the museum, which is free to view, non-paywalled, and
non-downloadable — the data is rendered as skeletal playback, not redistributed
as files. If a blanket permission is not something you can give, I would welcome
any terms you prefer: a specific subset, a written credit line, a time-limited
arrangement, or a licence fee.

**What we would commit to in return:**

- Full citation of the *Data in Brief* paper and the numediart Institute
  wherever the sequences appear, on-screen and not buried in a footer.
- A link back to the repository and the DOI from the exhibit itself.
- No redistribution: sequences are streamed as rendered motion, and the source
  files are not downloadable from our platform.
- Removal on request, at any time, no questions asked.

If it is useful to you, I would also be glad to share back anything we learn.
We are building toward capturing African martial arts — Dambe, Laamb, Engolo,
Nguni stick fighting and others — for which, as far as we can establish, no
motion dataset of any kind exists. Your methodology is the reference we are
working from, and we would be happy to discuss our capture protocol with your
group, or to make our own captures available to you on the same terms you extend
to us.

I am happy to send a private link to the museum so you can see the context
before deciding, and equally happy to remove the sequence immediately if you
would prefer we not use it at all.

With thanks for the dataset and for the work behind it,

Kenneth Moody
Plajah — plajah.com
