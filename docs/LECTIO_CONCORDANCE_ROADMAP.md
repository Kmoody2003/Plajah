# Lectio concordance and comparative study

The target is scholarly Bible study at least comparable to Logos, with Plajah's Sacred Library, history resources, and creation tools providing distinctive workflows. The implemented milestone below is substantial working infrastructure; it is not a claim of Logos feature parity.

## Shipped foundation

Lectio's study workbench now includes Concordance, Word study, Originals, History, Compare, and Notebook alongside the existing cross references, notes, and download search. It is available on narrow screens as a dismissible study panel.

Concordance uses an in-memory token posting index over hydrated Bible chapters. The complete bundled KJV hydrates all 31,102 verses for reading and concordance, with an IndexedDB snapshot for subsequent offline use. Its retained CrossWire metadata identifies the digital distribution as GPL and grants use for any purpose; the asset manifest preserves attribution and the upstream source repository. It supports exact words and phrases, all/any words, prefixes, exclusions, accent/vowel-mark folding, testament/book/chapter filters, complete paginated results, occurrence summaries, and export of all matches with edition and scope. Hebrew combining marks stay attached to their words. Offline coverage and downloads respect testament-specific translations; KJV John 3 fallback cannot substitute for another edition.

Word study searches 5,742 Greek and 8,675 Hebrew public-domain Strong’s dictionary entries, bundled with the provider’s edition metadata. Selected passages now show CrossWire KJV 3.1 English phrase groups with source-provided Strong’s identifiers across all 31,102 verses. Clicking a group opens dictionary lookup and a complete tagged-group concordance with book filters, counts and pagination. Multiple identifiers, source Greek forms, source positions and raw grammatical codes remain intact. Tags explicitly belong to the KJV when another translation is active. Counts measure tagged English groups rather than original-language token frequency. These CrossWire grammar codes remain distinct from the original-text parsing used in Originals.

Originals adds a separate source-language reading and research panel. The OSHB/WLC 4.20 snapshot contains 39 Hebrew/Aramaic book files, 23,213 source verses and 305,507 main word tokens, with 1,278 variant word tokens preserved separately. The derived UGNT 0.34 snapshot contains 27 Greek books, 7,958 source verses and 137,990 main tokens. Unicode is retained without normalization; Hebrew word boundaries and component slashes remain in the data. Source selectors allow independent chapter and verse reading. The selected translation appears beside the source text when available. Source edition identity, revision and license follow saved passages into the notebook.

OSHB's official VerseMap.xml supplies KJV-to-WLC shifts, including Psalm superscriptions and partial verse relations. Partial mappings show the whole source verse as context and do not claim a complete equivalence. Search-result navigation requires a unique non-partial mapping. Greek uses its ufw source references, with source-checked editorial exceptions at the end of 2 Corinthians, the end of 3 John, and the Revelation 12/13 boundary. Their rationale and source identities are retained in `public/sacred/greek/verse-map.json`; these are Plajah editorial mappings, not publisher-issued mappings. Shared locators do not imply equality with the KJV's Textus Receptus. Multi-source relationships provide direct source-reading buttons. Source bracketed readings and footnotes remain visible. Hebrew ketiv and qere remain separate, with both available for word inspection. Main-word indexes deliberately exclude qere variants.

Original word inspection decodes OSHB Hebrew/Aramaic grammar and the UGNT/CNTR Greek nine-slot scheme into readable labels, with short explanations of selected terms and retained raw codes. Unsupported values stay visible rather than being guessed. Verb aspect is not reduced to automatic English tense. Complete per-corpus lemma and morphology indexes support exact lemma search, exact source parsing search, intersection at the same token, book filters and pagination. These are actual original-token counts, distinct from KJV tagged-English-group counts. Extended Greek source identifiers remain unconverted until an audited identifier map is supplied. Hebrew numeric source lemmas can open the existing Strong dictionary.

History contains ten sourced entries covering inscriptions, palace reliefs, manuscript witnesses, and architecture. Examples include Mesha, Sennacherib’s prism, Lachish, the Cyrus Cylinder, the Qumran manuscripts, and Codex Sinaiticus. A chapter view and searchable chronological collection preserve date ranges, dating basis, find or production context, catalog identifiers, observations, interpretive limits, study questions, and institutional links. Passage associations are editorial relevance links, not claims that an object proves a verse.

Compare searches bundled Qur’an, Gita, Dhammapada, and legacy Buddhist excerpts, plus cached Jewish and Sikh sections. Matching words identify candidates for reading; the software does not infer doctrinal agreement or historical dependence. Saved Bible passages, other-faith passages, and historical objects can be paired in a shared device-local notebook. Relationship types, similarities, differences, and context remain separate, with comparisons explicitly labeled personal interpretations. JSON import validates links and evidence references; exports preserve citations and edition credits. Legacy quick Buddhist comparison notes remain available.

## Readers in the faith sections

Each live faith section opens its own reader. References and divisions retain the tradition or edition’s structure.

| Section | Available texts | Current coverage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Christianity | Existing Lectio Bible translations | Complete bundled KJV: 66 books, 1,189 chapters, 31,102 verses. Other translations load on demand; 66-book numbering remains the baseline |
| Islam | Qur’an, Pickthall English; optional Quran Uthmani Arabic | All 114 surahs and 6,236 ayahs bundled in English; Arabic on demand for the current session |
| Judaism | Tanakh Hebrew and JPS 1917 English via Sefaria; additional Sefaria references | Source-specific chapters; other text references select available reusable English editions where possible |
| Hinduism | Bhagavad Gita, Edwin Arnold’s poetic rendering | All 18 chapters bundled; paragraphs are not Sanskrit verse numbers |
| Sikhism | Sri Guru Granth Sahib Ji via GurbaniNow | All 1,430 ang pages addressable on demand; Gurmukhi, transliteration, and supplied English translation |
| Buddhism | Complete Max Müller Dhammapada, plus the existing excerpt catalog | All 423 Dhammapada verses in 26 chapters; nine paired verse groups retain the source edition’s numbering |

Readers support passage-linked notes, research bookmarks, section navigation, cached-work search with pagination, full-work download/resume, and explicit source failures. Notes are device-local. Cached sections persist in IndexedDB; bundled edition files receive production service-worker runtime caching after first access. The first visit still needs the installed application or a network connection. API availability and browser cross-origin permissions remain external dependencies.

The downloaded public editions retain their source files, rights statements, and a SHA-256 manifest in `public/sacred/manifest.json`. Sefaria edition titles, license strings, and edition sources accompany the text. GurbaniNow’s default translation is labeled as provider-supplied because its page response does not identify a translator. The legacy Heart Sutra/excerpt catalog still needs an editorial provenance audit. Public availability is not treated as evidence of public-domain status.

## Next delivery stages

1. Corpus integrity: audited edition identifiers, attribution, rights, source links, canon/versification mappings, checksums, and explicit coverage. The built-in John 3 fallback is now restricted to KJV; audit and invalidate any older mislabeled caches. Support Catholic and Orthodox canons with source-specific mappings rather than assuming 66 books everywhere.
2. Indexed concordance: persist/version the token postings, add an explicit Boolean expression grammar and cross-translation queries, and benchmark a full Bible corpus on low-memory devices. Pagination, testament/chapter filters, accent-aware matching, exclusions, and cited result export are now implemented.
3. Original languages: source corpora, readable grammar, original-token lemma/parsing queries, OSHB verse mappings and KJV phrase-to-Strong alignment are implemented. Next add morphology feature filters and a query grammar, accent-aware original-form search, an audited extended Greek identifier map, broader lexicons, alternative original editions, reviewed reverse interlinear links and apparatus witnesses. Exact source parsing search currently treats attached prefixes and suffixes as part of the complete code. Never manufacture alignment from an English word. Validate counts against fixed scholarly corpora.
4. Passage study: import the full cross-reference apparatus, add literary context, people/places/events, manuscript variant apparatus, commentary retrieval, and translation-difference alignment. Citation-preserving notebooks and a historical evidence model are implemented; expand their reviewed content and preserve the distinction between editorial coverage and exhaustive coverage.
5. Comparative corpus: extend the initial reader editions with more verified translations, commentaries, Hindu texts, Buddhist canons, and source-native context browsing. Distinguish shared Jewish/Christian text from distinct interpretation, scripture from commentary, and school-specific teaching from tradition-wide claims. Provide supported edition refresh and stronger snapshot/version migration rather than silently changing a saved research citation.
6. Comparative evidence graph: typed relations for shared wording, ethical analogy, contrasting doctrine, shared narrative, and proposed historical influence. Every relation records passages, edition, context, rationale, author/reviewer, and status. Similarity must never imply historical dependence or doctrinal agreement. AI proposals remain explicitly unreviewed with passage evidence until editorial review.
7. Plajah workflows: move cited study selections into Lectio reflection, teaching preparation, Ambo presentation, and media creation while preserving translation and source credits. Add accessible visual exploration of connections and differences, source-linked collaborative annotations, and controlled sharing of notebooks.

## Acceptance criteria

Exact concordance counts agree with independent corpus checks; no cap silently alters totals. Failed downloads and translation substitutes never count as the requested edition. All comparison claims are inspectable against the cited texts. Missing traditions, incomplete texts, and disputed readings remain visible. Reviewer assessments must represent internal diversity rather than flattening a religion to one proposition. Test offline indexing, storage failure, language normalization, canon mapping, and corpus updates before claiming production parity.


### Catholic canon and Orthodox book distinctions (October 6, 2026)
Lectio now includes all 73 Catholic books in bundled Douay–Rheims (Challoner), 1,334 chapters and 35,808 verse records. Provider omissions in 1 Kings 17:19, Proverbs 30:29 and Baruch 6:37 are restored with provenance in the asset. Catholic Esther/Daniel additions and Baruch 6 are integrated using this edition’s numbering. Concordance filters include the deuterocanonical books.
The responsive dropdown labels shared books in slate, shared Catholic/Orthodox material in gold and additional Orthodox material in purple. The existing remote Greek Septuagint collection has explicit appendix/anthology labels and a notice that Orthodox traditions vary; it is not a universal Orthodox canon or a bundled English Orthodox edition. Separate Susanna, Bel and the Dragon and Letter of Jeremiah are identified as shared Catholic material. Catholic/Orthodox note keys are separated from legacy KJV keys. Verse-linked original language and history tools remain restricted to their supported KJV reference basis.
