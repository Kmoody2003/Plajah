# Little Fox, Big Trees
*For ages 5-7  |  Linocut / woodblock print  |  340 words  |  11 pages (spreads) incl. cover and back*

**One line:** A small fox who is afraid of the towering forest finds the courage to help a lost fawn, and the trees turn out to be gentle.
**Theme:** Courage is helping someone else while you are still scared
**Refrain the child can join in on:** "Left foot, right foot, hush."
**Back-cover blurb:** Tam is a little fox, and tonight Tam must walk home through the biggest trees in the world, alone. Then something small steps out from behind a trunk, and it needs Tam's help.

## Characters
- **Tam**, little fox (hero). careful, brave in small steps, kind, honest about being scared. *Voice:* Whispers when scared, talks plainly when brave. *Signature:* Counts their steps out loud: "Left foot, right foot, hush." *Arc:* Starts afraid of being small; ends knowing small is no reason not to help.
- **Dot**, spotted fawn (companion). lost, polite, surprisingly funny, trusting. *Voice:* Soft and polite: "Hello. I am small too." *Signature:* Says "I am small too" and blinks slowly. *Arc:* Starts lost; ends reunited with her family and a new friend.
- **Mama Fox**, grown fox (supporting). warm, steady, proud. *Voice:* Calm, short sentences. *Signature:* Leaves a lantern of fireflies at the den door. *Arc:* Waits at the den; understands without being told.
- **The Big Trees**, towering trunks (chorus). silent, watchful, gentle. *Voice:* Never speak. They hold the light. *Signature:* A gold, sleepy light in every trunk at sunset. *Arc:* Look frightening; end up looking like kind giants keeping watch.

## Questions to ask while reading together
1. Why was Tam afraid of the Big Trees at the start?
2. What changed when Dot stepped out from behind the tree?
3. Is it possible to be brave and scared at the same time? When?
4. What did Tam mean by "friends who stand very still"?

## Pokee's editor notes (AI second opinion; already acted on where noted in docs/SHOWCASE_BOOKS.md)
- **Structure:** The narrative arc is clear and effective for the target age. The transition from fear to courage is driven by empathy (helping Dot), which aligns perfectly with the stated theme. The resolution provides a satisfying emotional shift in Tam's perception of the environment.
- **Best line (Pokee's pick):** Left foot, right foot, hush.
- **Suggestion, spread 3 (reword):** The line 'Tam counted the trunks: one, two, three, four... then lost count' is a bit prosaic compared to the poetic rhythm of the rest of the book. Consider a more sensory or rhythmic description of the overwhelming number of trees to maintain the lyrical quality.
- **Suggestion, spread 8 (reword):** The phrase 'gold, sleepy light' is lovely, but 'sleepy' might be slightly abstract for the youngest end of the 5-7 range. 'Soft' or 'warm' might be more immediately visual, though 'sleepy' works well for the mood.
