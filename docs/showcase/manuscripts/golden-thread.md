# The Golden Thread
*For ages 7-9  |  Folk papercut with gold  |  694 words  |  14 pages (spreads) incl. cover and back*

**One line:** A weaver whose thread never runs out follows it through three kind tasks to a tangled spirit at the top of the world, and learns what the thread is made of.
**Theme:** Generosity that does not count the cost, and what we are willing to give away
**Refrain the child can join in on:** "Tug, tug, tug."
**Back-cover blurb:** Mira the weaver has a golden thread that never runs out. One night it gives a tug and leads her over the hills, to a wolf, a river and the moon, and finally to the top of the world, where she must decide what a gift is really worth.

## Characters
- **Mira**, weaver girl of Three Hills (hero). patient, generous, brave in quiet ways, never counts. *Voice:* Quiet and thoughtful; speaks to the thread as if it can hear her. *Signature:* Threads a needle slowly and says, "One small stitch at a time." *Arc:* Starts as a village weaver who gives without thinking; ends knowing what her gift was made of.
- **Glint**, golden thread spirit (companion). playful, tangled, old and tired, secretly shy. *Voice:* Speaks only in tugs until the top of the world; then in a warm, rusty voice. *Signature:* Tug, tug, tug. *Arc:* Starts as a mysterious guide; ends untangled and free.
- **The Grey Wolf**, old wolf with a torn coat (supporting). proud, lonely, grateful. *Voice:* Gruff and slow. *Signature:* Looks away when he is thanked. *Arc:* Hides at the edge of the forest; ends mended and no longer alone.
- **The Moon**, sleepy moon (supporting). tired, kind, a little vain about her light. *Voice:* A yawn of a voice. *Signature:* Yawns when she is sad. *Arc:* Her light is slipping; Mira ties her sash and her light returns.

## Questions to ask while reading together
1. Why did the thread seem heavier as Mira gave more of it away?
2. What were the three tasks? What did each one teach Mira?
3. Why did Glint need thread that was "given freely"? What would be different if she had given it to get something back?
4. Is there a time you gave something and ended up with more than before?

## Words to know
- **unravelled**: Came apart, thread by thread.
- **tangled**: Twisted together in a knot that is hard to undo.
- **mend**: To fix something that is torn or broken.
- **sash**: A long band of cloth worn around the waist or shoulder.
- **freely**: Without asking for anything back.

## Pokee's editor notes (AI second opinion; already acted on where noted in docs/SHOWCASE_BOOKS.md)
- **Structure:** The narrative arc is clear and emotionally resonant. The progression from passive giving (village) to active questing (tasks) to ultimate sacrifice (the spool) works well for the target age. The thematic link between the physical mending and the spiritual 'untangling' is handled simply but effectively.
- **Best line (Pokee's pick):** Thread that is given is thread that comes back.
- **Suggestion, spread 6 (reword):** The line 'It should be lighter' is a bit logical for a 7-year-old to grasp immediately in the flow of the story. Consider changing to 'But why does it feel so heavy?' to make the physical sensation more immediate and less abstract.
- **Suggestion, spread 10 (add):** Add a small physical detail of Mira's hesitation or fear before she unwinds the spool. For example, 'Her hands trembled for a moment.' This heightens the bravery of the act and makes the sacrifice feel more real to the reader.
- **Suggestion, spread 12 (reword):** The activity instructions are clear, but 'Trace each one with your finger' might be too passive. Consider 'Follow the golden path with your finger to help Mira find her way home' to make it more interactive and goal-oriented.
