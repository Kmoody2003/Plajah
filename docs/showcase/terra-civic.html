How drafting works in Forum

Private while it's written. Public when it's ready.

The office keeps its working copy to itself. Each time it publishes, people get a new public version, the changes from the last one, and a place to weigh in. Public versions can never be quietly edited.

The Civic Record

A public record anyone can check.

Laws, votes, notices and awards go into an append-only log. It uses the same method that keeps web security certificates honest, with Bitcoin used only as an independent clock.

The official signs

The clerk certifies the ordinance with their own key. Plajah only runs the log.

It's logged for good

Records are added, never edited. A correction points back to the original.

Witnesses co-sign

Libraries, universities and newsrooms confirm the log only ever grew.

A public clock stamps it

A free OpenTimestamps proof ties the record to a Bitcoin block.

You check it

Tap the badge and your phone re-runs every check itself.

Ground rules

What Terra Civic will and won't do.

Elected officials keep the vote.

Public signals are advisory and verified. They are never presented as an election.

ARIA explains, it never decides.

Counts and scores come from code. ARIA summarizes, cites sources and never edits a bill on its own.

Every copy says what it is.

Hosted law is labelled unofficial, names its source and links to the official text.

Moderation is viewpoint-neutral.

Published rules apply to everyone, and offices can respond to comments but not delete them.

Governments decide their programs.

Plajah supplies the tools. Anyone eligible can apply without joining Plajah.

Revenue comes back to the community.

A published share of government revenue funds Plajah's creators, charities and small businesses.
