# law-evidence: claims needing expert review

General: some distractor options were mechanically padded with a neutral phrase ("under the usual federal approach", "in a typical federal proceeding", etc.) to remove an answer-length tell; a few read slightly awkwardly and could be tidied. Rule text is paraphrased from memory of the FRE as restyled (2011) and amended through 2024; please check against the current official text.

- l01: Bourjaily v. United States (1987) cited for the preponderance standard under 104(a); holding is as stated, but confirm that the 2000 amendment to 801(d)(2) (statement alone insufficient) is correctly described in l06.
- l01, l10: Bruton described as covering a "nontestifying codefendant's confession naming the defendant"; Richardson v. Marsh (1987) and Gray v. Maryland (1998) redaction rules stated from memory.
- l02: Old Chief v. United States (1997) facts (assault causing serious bodily injury) and the limited scope of the holding.
- l03: Michelson v. United States (1948) facts (arrest question to character witnesses in a bribery-type prosecution); the "have you heard" / "did you know" form convention.
- l04: Huddleston v. United States (1988) described as a 104(b) sufficiency test; the exact list of Rule 412 criminal exceptions; scope of Rule 408 (including the criminal-case carve-out, not discussed).
- l05: Mutual Life Insurance Co. v. Hillmon (1892) cited for state-of-mind use of statements of intent; the federal position that nonassertive conduct is not a "statement" (Advisory Committee note).
- l06: 801(d)(1)(B) 2014 amendment (rehabilitation on other grounds); Tome v. United States (1995) premotive requirement; Bourjaily holding.
- l07: Exact wording of 803(6) (post-2014 "opponent does not show lack of trustworthiness" burden allocation), 803(8)(A)(ii) law-enforcement limit, 803(18) treatise handling, 803(22) felony threshold. 803(16) ancient documents not discussed.
- l08: Williamson v. United States (1994) holding; 804(b)(6) intent language and Giles v. California (2008) link; 2019 amendment to Rule 807 (notice requirement; "sufficient guarantees of trustworthiness" with corroboration); 804(a) categories.
- l09: Crawford v. Washington (2004) facts and holding; Davis v. Washington (2006) (Hammon companion); Michigan v. Bryant (2011) description of the ongoing-emergency analysis; Ohio v. Clark (2015) facts (three-year-old, preschool teachers); Giles v. California (2008).

Illinois (2012) "no majority rationale"; Melendez-Diaz (2009), Bullcoming (2011) holdings; the statement that business records are generally nontestimonial.
- l11: Exact 901(b) subsections and 902 numbering, including 902(13) and (14) (2017 amendments) and notice requirements; handling of chain-of-custody as weight versus admissibility.
- l12: Rule 1001-1008 subsection details (1004(c) opponent in control; 1008 jury questions); the passing claim that courts split on edge cases involving testimony from personal knowledge versus a recording of the same event.
- l13: Rule 606(b) wording after the 2006 amendment (mistake in entering the verdict on the verdict form); Tanner v. United States (1987) (intoxication not an outside influence); Pena-Rodriguez v. Colorado (2017) scope; Rule 615 exceptions list; state wide-open cross rule claim.
- l14: United States v. Abel (1984) facts (secret prison gang); Luce v. United States (1984) and Ohler v. United States (2000); Rule 609 details (ten-year clock, notice, 609(d) narrow juvenile exception); Rule 608(b) privilege sentence; the claim that theft crimes usually do not qualify under 609(a)(2).
- l15: Rule 701(c) added in 2000; Rule 704(b) scope; Rule 703 balancing test wording.
- l16: Daubert (1993), Joiner (1997), Kumho Tire (1999) holdings; the December 1, 2023 amendment to Rule 702 and its Advisory Committee commentary; the list of states that retain Frye (California, Illinois, New York, Pennsylvania) as of this writing is from memory and may be out of date, and several of these have partial or evolving positions.
