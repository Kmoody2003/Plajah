# law-g35 uncertain claims (for human expert review)

- l03: Brown v. Board (1954) described as holding racially separate public schools "unequal and not allowed"; simplified wording of the holding. Fourteenth Amendment as source of "equal protection" is standard.
- l04: Tinker v. Des Moines (1969): armbands protesting a war, "substantial disruption" standard simplified. Statement that true threats and incitement are less protected is simplified.
- l06: veto-override rule (two-thirds of both chambers) is standard; bill-to-law steps simplified (omits pocket veto, conference committee detail, signing deadline).
- l07: Marbury v. Madison (1803) described as first use of judicial review; "Senate approves federal judges" is correct but simplified.
- l08: local government structure varies by state; claim that school boards are often elected "in many places" is hedged but unverified as a percentage.
- l09: civil/criminal plaintiff/defendant/prosecutor terminology simplified; mediation described generally.
- l11: Gideon v. Wainwright (1963) simplified to "serious crime"; the Court's holding concerned felony defendants in state court. In re Gault (1967): notice of charges and lawyer are correct, but it also covered confrontation of witnesses and privilege against self-incrimination (omitted).
- l12: "three levels" in federal and state systems is a generalization (some states have two levels or no intermediate court). Supreme Court has nine justices (set by statute, currently nine).
- l14: "contracts of minors under eighteen can usually be cancelled" is the majority common-law rule (voidable); state age and exceptions (necessaries) vary. Statement that "some contracts must be in writing" refers to the statute of frauds, not named.
- l15: copyright described as requiring permission before copying and selling; simplified (fair use, automatic protection not mentioned).
- l16: IDEA reference (Individuals with Disabilities Education Act) correct but only named, not described in detail. "Compulsory education in every state until a certain age" generalized.
- l17: "customary international law" described loosely as "long-standing customs"; no opinio juris term used.
- l18: Antarctic Treaty 1959 (signed 1959, in force 1961) described as peaceful uses and science. US Senate two-thirds of senators present is correct for treaties; executive agreements are not covered. Vienna Convention on the Law of Treaties 1969 and pacta sunt servanda are correct but US is not a party to the Vienna Convention (not stated in the lesson).
- l19: UN founded 1945; "almost every recognized country is a member" (193 members, not stated). Security Council: 15 members, five permanent (China, France, Russia, UK, US) correct. ICJ in The Hague correct. Statement that the UDHR is not itself binding is the mainstream view; some scholars argue parts have become customary law. Headquarters in New York correct.
- l20: CRC adopted by the UN General Assembly in 1989; "almost every country has joined" (United States is the notable non-party; verify current status as of ratifications). US signed in 1995, not ratified: believed correct. Characterization of US critics' concerns (parental rights, sovereignty) is a summary of common arguments.
