## Verifier-flagged changes

1. **L9 ALI "affirmative consent" (verifier B was right).** Was: revised Article 213 "defines offenses by reference to the absence of affirmative consent". Now: ALI approved the revised Article at its 2022 Annual Meeting; its consent definition (Council, Oct 2016) is willingness to engage in a specific act, "express or ... inferred from behavior, both action and inaction", no resistance required, a clear "No/Stop/Don't" establishes lack of consent. Lesson states that the membership amended the definition in 2022 and the final published wording was not checked. Sources opened: https://www.ali.org/news/articles/updated-consent-definition (text of 213.0(3)); https://www.ali.org/projects/show/sexual-assault-and-related-offenses (2022 meeting actions; "motion to amend the definition of consent passed"). Worked example "In every modern code" softened to "In modern codes".
2. **L7 Redline (verifier B was right).** Was: Redline cited for police/victim killing "a bystander or a co-felon". Now: Redline (Pa. 1958) is a co-felon shot by police; People v. Washington (Cal. 1965) is an accomplice shot by the robbery victim. No bystander case is cited since none was confirmed. Anchor added; l07.q5 explanation updated. Sources: https://people.brandeis.edu/~teuber/redline.html (full opinion; also confirms Pennsylvania's 1794 Act was the first to divide murder into degrees); https://law.justia.com/cases/california/supreme-court/2d/62/777.html (Washington).
3. **L11 burglary example (verifier B was right).** Was: entry "through an unlocked door" not a breaking, contradicting the lesson's rule that pushing open a closed door is a breaking. Now: the door was "standing wide open"; lesson adds that pushing open a closed (even unlocked) door is a breaking. Also added MPC 221.1(3) (no convictions for both burglary and the intended crime unless the other offense is a first- or second-degree felony) and corrected MPC 220.1 (arson covers destroying a building or insuring-fraud property; reckless endangerment is separate 220.1(2)). Sources: MPC text at https://www.criminallawweb.net/web1/mpc/mpc.html (221.1, 220.1); https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/burglary.
