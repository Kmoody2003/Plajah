# med-history: items for expert review

- l01: Edwin Smith Papyrus date (c. 1600 BCE, copy of older material), Ebers Papyrus (c. 1550 BCE); the three-way verdict format of the Smith cases; Esagil-kin-apli attributed to the 11th century BCE; Hammurabi's surgeon penalties (c. 18th century BCE).
- l02: "some sixty texts" in the Hippocratic Corpus; Galen born 129 CE; claim that Galen described a rete mirabile in the human brain (it is found in some animals, such as ungulates); the pores-in-the-septum belief; Hippocratic Oath prohibitions (abortifacients) described as in the "ancient form".
- l03: al-Razi dates (c. 854 to 925), Ibn Sina (980 to 1037), Canon translated into Latin in the 12th century; waqf funding of bimaristans; "separate wards, pharmacies, teaching" generalized; extent to which Ibn al-Nafis's pulmonary transit was known in Europe (said to be debated); Canon discussing "contagion by invisible agents" is a common but contested reading.
- l04: Black Death mortality "a third to a half"; Ragusa 1377 thirty days then Venice forty days; Vesalius's illustrators ("circle of Titian's workshop", often attributed to Jan van Calcar); specific Galen errors named (breastbone structure is stated loosely, and the septum pores point is the safer claim).
- l05: Malpighi capillaries 1661 in frog lung; Leeuwenhoek 1670s to 1680s; Laennec 1816; Lind 1747 twelve sailors in pairs; Sydenham "English Hippocrates".
- l06: Snow handle removal (outbreak already declining); Semmelweis details (Vienna 1847, chlorinated lime, Kolletschka implied but not named, died 1865); Koch dates (anthrax 1876, TB 1882, cholera 1883); Lister 1867.
- l07: Crawford Long 1842 (published later); Morton at MGH 16 October 1846 with John Collins Warren; Simpson chloroform 1847; Snow gave chloroform to Queen Victoria 1853; Halsted gloves in 1890s; Landsteiner 1901; Gibbon 1953; Murray 1954 identical twins; Barnard 1967.
- l08: variolation spread (Asia, Africa, Middle East); Montagu around 1721; Boylston and Cotton Mather in Boston 1721; Jenner 1796 and 1798; Meister 1885; smallpox last natural case 1977 Somalia, declared eradicated 1980; Salk trial result announced 1955; Cutter incident 1955; Sabin licensed 1961; as of this writing wild polio endemic in a few countries and vaccine-derived cases. Check current status.
- l09: Salvarsan 1910; Domagk Prontosil 1935, Nobel 1939 with Nazi prohibition; 1937 sulfanilamide elixir "more than a hundred" deaths; 1938 FD&C Act; Fleming 1928; Florey, Chain and Heatley, first patient 1941; enough for troops by 1944; Waksman streptomycin 1943 and the word "antibiotic"; Fleming's Nobel-lecture warning about underdosing.
- l10: Nightingale at Scutari 1854 and school 1860 at St Thomas'; Flexner Report 1910 (Carnegie Foundation); Copenhagen 1952 and Bjorn Ibsen; MRC streptomycin trial 1948 as "first properly randomized" (often so called, but contested); evidence-based medicine named in the 1990s; Medicare and Medicaid 1965.
- l11: Blackwell 1849; Garrett Anderson 1865 via Society of Apothecaries; Crumpler 1864 (New England Female Medical College); James McCune Smith 1837 Glasgow (claim "first Black American physician with a medical degree"); number of Black medical schools before and after Flexner (two remained: Howard and Meharry); Sims's fistula work 1840s on Anarcha, Lucey and Betsey; 1977 FDA guideline excluding women of childbearing potential; NIH Revitalization Act 1993.
- l12: Chadwick 1842; Public Health Act 1848; Great Stink 1858 and Bazalgette; Jersey City chlorination 1908; Grand Rapids fluoridation 1945; Doll and Hill 1950, Wynder and Graham 1950; 1964 Surgeon General's report; oral rehydration "many millions" of lives (figure not stated precisely).
- l13: 1918 influenza H1N1, "roughly fifty million" deaths (estimates vary widely), virus identified in the 1930s, 1918 genome reconstructed in early 2000s; claim that early and sustained measures correlated with better outcomes; HIV recognized 1981, virus identified 1983 and 1984 (Barre-Sinoussi, Montagnier, Gallo), zidovudine 1987, combination therapy mid 1990s.
- l14: Mendel 1866; Franklin and Wilkins credit statement; Sanger sequencing 1977; Mullis PCR in the 1980s; HGP started 1990, draft 2000, complete 2003; gap-free sequence 2022; CF gene 1989; imatinib approved 2001; Gelsinger 1999 at Penn.
- l15: bloodletting "about two thousand years"; Pierre Louis's findings on bloodletting in pneumonia (stated as "no benefit"; the actual claims were more nuanced); thalidomide withdrawal 1961 and 1962; Kelsey's reasons; Kefauver-Harris 1962 including informed consent rules; Moniz Nobel 1949, Freeman transorbital method, "tens of thousands" of lobotomies; WHI estrogen plus progestin arm stopped 2002; rofecoxib withdrawal 2004; Marshall and Warren early 1980s, Nobel 2005.
