# law-intl-humanitarian-criminal: claims for expert review

- l01: ICJ Nicaragua (1986) statement that only the "most grave" forms of force are an armed attack; paraphrase of AP I preamble wording on equal application.
- l02: US signed (1977) but has not ratified AP I and II; "participation essentially universal" for the 1949 Conventions; ICJ 1996 nuclear opinion cited for the Martens clause; characterisation of the US response to the ICRC customary law study.
- l03: ICJ later reaffirming "effective control" for attribution (Bosnia Genocide 2007) versus Tadic "overall control"; AP II threshold wording.
- l04: statement that states say only "clearly excessive" imbalance violates proportionality (treaty text says "excessive"; Rome Statute says "clearly excessive").
- l05: Article numbers for Convention I (12, 15, 21, 22) and AP I (40, 41, 42); red crystal "same legal value" claim.
- l06: AP I Art 44 summary of the relaxed distinction rule; Convention III Articles 5, 12, 13, 17, 118; Hamdan v. Rumsfeld (2006) holding as stated (common Art 3 applicable; commissions not "regularly constituted").
- l07: DRC v. Uganda (2005) finding that Uganda was an occupying power in Ituri and responsible for looting and failures to prevent; 2004 Wall Advisory Opinion treating settlements as contrary to Art 49(6) and Israel's contrary position. The July 2024 ICJ advisory opinion on the occupied Palestinian territory is deliberately not covered.
- l08: Convention-in-miniature framing of common Art 3; Protocol II Article numbers (4, 5, 6, 7-12, 13-18, 14, 17); ICRC reading of Art 6(5) as excluding war crimes suspects; the three common theories of how armed groups are bound.
- l09: BWC "no standing verification body"; Protocol IV (1995) on blinding lasers; ICJ 1996 opinion wording and the unanimous disarmament holding; TPNW dates (adopted 2017, in force 2021).
- l10: Rome Statute extension of the starvation crime to non-international conflicts by the Assembly in 2019 (date and form); UNSC Res 2417 (2018); Al Mahdi sentence of nine years (2016); AP I Art 70 and "agreement of the parties concerned" wording; ENMOD thresholds ("widespread, long-lasting or severe").
- l11: ICRC Interpretive Guidance 2009 three-part test and "continuous combat function"; characterisation of state positions on cyber effects and of the CCW/UNGA discussion of autonomous weapons "as of this writing"; Tallinn Manual description.
- l12: grave breaches list for Convention IV Art 147 and AP I Art 85 summarised; Art 146 wording; DRC v. Belgium (2002) holding summarised including the "four routes" to accountability; AP I Art 91 and Hague IV Art 3 compensation; Protocol I fact-finding commission "little use".
- l13: IMT numbers (24 indicted, 22 tried, 12 death sentences, 3 acquittals) and judgment dates; Tokyo judgments in 1948 and Pal's dissent; seven Nuremberg Principles (ILC 1950); In re Yamashita (US, 1946) description and criticism; the Tribunal's rejection of the nullum crimen objection as paraphrased.
- l14: Tadic (1999) JCE categories; Celebici trial 1998 and appeal 2001 on effective control; Bemba Gombo trial 2016, appeal reversal 2018 and the stated reasoning about "reasonable measures"; Rome Statute Arts 25, 26, 27, 28, 29, 31, 33 paraphrases.
- l15: Article 8(2)(b)(iv) "clearly excessive" and knowledge; Lubanga trial 2012, appeal 2014; Art 8(2)(d) and (f) threshold summary; claim that "participation" extends to roles exposing children to danger.
- l16: Art 7 list and 7(2)(a) policy requirement; ILC draft articles on crimes against humanity (2019) and absence of a convention "as of this writing"; Bosnia v. Serbia (2007) holdings and Croatia v. Serbia (2015).
- l17: ICTY Res 827 (1993), ICTR Res 955 (1994); Karadzic life sentence on appeal 2019; Mladic 2017 and 2021; Krstic appeal 2004; Taylor 2012 and 2013; ECCC 2018 genocide convictions (Vietnamese and Cham groups); the ICTY decision not to open an investigation into NATO 1999; Akayesu holding on rape.
- l18: approximate number of states parties (about 125); status of withdrawals; Kampala amendment details (Arts 8 bis, 15 bis, 15 ter), activation date 17 July 2018 and the exact scope of the opt-out and non-party limits; Art 77 sentence maximum; eighteen judges; Darfur Res 1593 (2005) and Libya Res 1970 (2011).
- l19: Art 17, 18, 19, 20 summaries; Art 98(1) reading; Al-Bashir warrants (2009 and 2010) and the 2019 Jordan Appeals Chamber ruling on head-of-state immunity; the criticism attributed to commentators.
- l20: SCSL Kallon and Kamara decision (2004); Koblenz 2022 life sentence; Russia/Ukraine (2023) and Israel/Hamas (2024) warrants described in neutral terms but developing; US sanctions and positions on the Court; Colombian transitional justice model; the claim that Rome Statute is "silent" on amnesties.
- General: some questions were written to test nuance (e.g. l10.q5 "least likely violated"); reviewers should check that stem and keyed answer are unambiguous.
