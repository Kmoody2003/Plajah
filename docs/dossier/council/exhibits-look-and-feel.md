## The decision

**Lead:** The Classical Mind · **Counterpoint:** The Rebellious Hand · **Editor:** The Radical Minimalist

Build every exhibit as one walked axis of rooms on a single shared grid, with a persistent plan strip (left on laptop and TV, bottom sheet on phone) that always says 'Room 3 of 9', and one Room template: archive photograph largest and ungraded with its real edges and full credit, reconstruction smaller, framed, tagged RECONSTRUCTION with a one-line note on what is evidence and what is imagined. Type: a real serif for titles in sentence case set whole, a plain sans for all system voice, 18px minimum body on phone and 28px on TV, and the tiny tracked gold caps and the ghost numeral retired. Ford is three named wings on the axis (Motor City, the War Effort, Beliefs and Morals), with Beliefs a full room with its own door, in the same type, same size and same plain grammar as the Rouge, with real printed Dearborn Independent pages as the evidence and no lighting drama. Motion: entrance cut to about four seconds, skippable by any key, tap or remote button, with Enter live from frame one; rooms change with a short horizontal slide and the plan advances with it; reduced motion means cuts and 150ms fades; TV Back returns to the previous room.

**Kept from the counterpoint:** Reconstructions are treated as arguments, not windows: each flips (or toggles) to show what it rests on, the source photograph, document or inventory, and each exhibit gets its own material and one ink colour drawn from its subject (Detroit signal red-orange, iron-gall brown-black, lapis) so the family stops looking like one violet-sepia filter.

**The editor cut:** The Minimalist cut the tiny letterspaced gold labels, the ghost room numeral, the word-by-word title reveal, the violet-sepia grade and film grain, the outlined ENTER pills, and the Rebel's tilted taping, coffee rings and photocopy costume, which would not survive a twenty-minute read on a TV. RECONSTRUCTION is the only tracked-caps label left, so it is the only thing that shouts.

**Open decision for you:** Are you willing for Ford's Beliefs and Morals to be a full numbered room with its own door, at the same size and in the same voice as the Rouge and Willow Run, and for the lobby line 'He also put hatred in print' to come off the front of the card, so Motor City pride leads and the hard truth waits one honest room inside?

## The six proposals

### The Rebellious Hand — Evidence On The Wall

Build the whole family of exhibits as a wall of physical evidence, where every archive photograph is handled, taped, stamped and annotated by a human hand, and every painted reconstruction is visibly set apart as an argument rather than a window.

The lobby is one long horizontal wall you walk along, not three identical tall posters. Rooms are numbered strips on a single scrolling spine (a vertical rail on phone, a wall-wide timeline strip on laptop and TV) so you always see where you are, what is behind you and what is ahead. Ford's hard-truth section sits on the same spine as a dark, physically separate panel in the middle of his wall, at the same scale as the cars, never a footnote and never the cover.

### The Radical Minimalist — One Hairline, One Plate

Strip every exhibit to a single load-bearing contrast, the archive photograph against dark silence, and let one hairline and one numeral do all the wayfinding on phone, laptop and TV alike.

## What each director wrote to themselves afterwards

- **The Classical Mind:** I now hold that a plan strip saying 'Room 3 of 9' is the measure that every other element hangs from, and that it earns its authority only when the same Room template repeats exactly, so the Ford wing of Beliefs and Morals must sit in the same type, size and grammar as the Rouge, with printed Dearborn Independent pages as evidence and no lighting drama. I concede to the Rebel and the Baroque Dramatist that retiring the ghost numeral and the tiny gold caps costs the banner some atmosphere, but a numeral too faint to read was never a hierarchy, and the archive photograph's real edges now supply the rest the eye can trust.
- **The Rebellious Hand:** I now hold that in a history exhibit the evidence has to look rougher than the interpretation: the real Dearborn Independent page, with its real edges, credit and ink, goes largest and untouched, and the painted reconstruction is the thing that gets framed, tagged and apologised for. Beliefs gets the same plain door and type as the Rouge, because staging an ugly room as drama is itself a kind of polish; the rawness belongs in the printed page, not in the lighting.
- **The Futurist:** A persistent plan strip that reads 'Room 3 of 9' is the system rule I would have asked for, and I concede that the ghost numeral and the film-grain entrance were an old surface with a glow on it, so a four-second skippable entrance with Enter live from frame one is the right input-response behaviour. Where the walked axis still falls short for me is that the archive photograph and the RECONSTRUCTION tag are static template slots; I now hold that the evidence-versus-imagined boundary should itself be a state, such as a toggle that overlays what is sourced against what is painted, so the exhibit shows how it knows what it knows.
- **The World-Eclectic Traveler:** I now hold that a plain, shared grammar is itself a form of cultural respect: putting the Rouge and the Dearborn Independent pages in the same type, size and room template stops us treating Beliefs as spectacle, and shows 'ungraded, real edges, full credit' is the specificity I always asked for. I still say the Persia and Stele rooms need a second-script title and a named maker before the template ships, because a sans-and-serif system can quietly assume Latin.
- **The Baroque Dramatist:** For the Ford Beliefs room I now hold that withholding the lighting is the drama: when the evidence is real printed Dearborn Independent pages, any chiaroscuro I add would flatter the subject or the viewer's comfort, so the staging has to live in the order of rooms and the weight of the plain page itself. I also concede that a four-second entrance skippable from frame one, with Enter live immediately, is better timing than my fourteen-second curtain, because a reveal that holds the door shut on someone is a delay and not a reveal.
- **The Radical Minimalist:** Retiring the ghost numeral and the tracked gold caps showed me they were never emptiness, only faint decoration pretending to be silence; 'Room 3 of 9' in the plan strip says the same thing once, in plain sans, and that is the standard I now hold. I also concede to the Traveller that Beliefs and Morals earns a full door in identical grammar to the Rouge, because the real printed Dearborn Independent page carries the weight, and removing lighting drama there is what makes the evidence legible rather than a loss.
