# Christianity in Persia dossier: research notes

Prepared 2026-10-06 for specialist review. Files: `data/dossier/persia.ts`, `persiaAssets.json`, `persiaScenes.ts`, `persiaTimeline.ts`, `tests/dossierPersia.test.ts`.

Totals: 76 claims (22 established, 39 probable, 12 contested, 3 tradition), 49 ledger sources, 30 cleared image assets, 9 rooms, 21 nodes (each at 5 reading depths), 8 reconstruction scenes (no cast), 16 timeline milestones.

## Method and honest limits

- Web tools return summaries of pages, not always full text. Only two texts were read verbatim and are quoted: Acts 2:5-11 (KJV, via bible-api.com JSON) and James Legge's 1888 English translation of the Xi'an Stele (full OCR text from archive.org, searched with grep). Every other fact is paraphrased.
- Wikipedia articles were used as pointers to scholarship and to cross-check dates. They are cited in the ledger as secondary sources ("accessed 2026-10-06") and each claim that rests only on them is graded probable at most. A specialist should replace them with the underlying scholarship before public release.
- Several authoritative pages could not be fetched (Encyclopaedia Iranica returned HTTP 403 to direct fetch; UNESCO WHC 403; GEDSH returned connection resets for most entries; Duke dissertation page was a bot wall). Where Iranica or GEDSH is cited, the claim rests on a search-result excerpt and the ledger source says so.

## What was verified (strongest claims)

- Acts 2:9 wording (KJV) and that the text names hearers, not converts.
- Everything quoted from the Xi'an Stele: Olopun arrives at Chang'an in the ninth year of Chang-kwan (635); the 638 proclamation; the erection date line (second year of Chien-chung, 781, seventh day of the first month, Sunday); the Syriac notice (Seleucid 1092, Yazdbuzid of Kumdan, son of the late Meles of Balkh). The name is spelled "Meles" as in Legge.
- Perkins's own dates for the Urmia press and his recording of the Magi tradition.
- Constitution Articles 13, 14 and 64 (exact wording).
- That the 410 and 424 synods are recorded as described by the fourthcentury.com pages and GEDSH (Marutha entry).

## Not verified, and what was done about it

- Stele discovery date (1621-25 or 1625): sources differ; graded probable with a note.
- Identification of the stele author Jingjing with the Syriac "Adam": a scholarly inference; graded probable.
- Martyn details, Fiske Seminary founding, the Urmia output (NT 1846, OT 1852, "over eighty works"), New Julfa numbers and dates, Vank phases, the 1636 press, Catholic missions dates: all rest on Wikipedia summaries; graded probable or contested with notes.
- Sasanian-era seal date (British Museum "Early Sasanian"): the museum's own attribution; may be later.
- 1909 electoral law allocation (four minority seats; "two for Christians"): read only from search-result summaries; graded probable.
- Census series 1956-2016: taken from a Wikipedia table; should be checked against the Statistical Centre of Iran volumes.
- USCIRF arrest figures (171 in 2018 vs 16 in 2017) read in an Article 18 summary, not the USCIRF PDF.
- Genocide-era numbers (18,000 sheltered in mission compounds; 20-25,000 refugees; "tens of thousands" to Hamadan; 250,000 and 275,000 delegation claims; one source's 50,000 Armenians to Persia and 80,000 dead in northern Iran) are all secondary and contested. The exhibit text uses the delegation figures only as attributed political claims and never uses the 50,000/80,000 figures.
- Left out entirely because it could not be sourced: Pahlavi-era policy; Timur's Tikrit massacre; Armenian Apostolic traditions of Thaddeus and Bartholomew; UNESCO listing of the Armenian monastic ensembles; Sasanian Chronicle of Seert details; the Posi and Martha martyrdom (appears in a search summary of unknown provenance); quotations from Ishoyahb III (a Wikipedia-quoted phrase was not verified); the Sasanika/Nolt article on Persian Christian women (found, not read).

## Open questions for a specialist reviewer

1. Parthian-era Christianity: is "documentary evidence begins with the Sasanians" (c-parthian-gap) fair, given Syriac sources on Adiabene and Edessa?
2. Does the exhibit grade the Addai/Mari/Thomas traditions fairly (tradition, with the Doctrine of Addai graded as a text of known date)?
3. Chronology of Simeon bar Sabbae (340/341/344) and the status of the double-tax narrative.
4. Whether to describe the 424 canon as "independence" at all in public text.
5. Authenticity and dating of the Kyou Shooku (Tonko-Hikyu) Jingjiao manuscripts; and the Berlin Qocho fragment (683-770 vs 9th century).
6. New Julfa demographics (150,000 vs 500,000) and whether the Vank dates 1606 / 1655-64 are right.
7. The size and causes of 1915-1918 losses in Persian Azerbaijan, as distinct from Ottoman territory; the motive for the killing of Mar Shimun XIX.
8. Iranian apostasy law as applied to converts: is "capital crime, applied unevenly" an accurate summary of current practice? Review primary legal texts and a legal scholar.
9. Whether the exhibit should name the Chaldean and Assyrian reserved seat as "Assyrian and Chaldean" (as in Article 64) or split them.

- Mogao "Christian painting" restorations, Da Qin Pagoda (Church of the East link doubtful), Syriac "Mar Addai and Mari" icon (saints' faces), Tatev and other Armenian (Republic) sites (not Persia).
