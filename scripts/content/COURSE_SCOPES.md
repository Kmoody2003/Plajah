# Course scopes (use with AUTHOR_BRIEF.md)

## graphic-design — "Graphic Design", accent #7A2BD6, 24 lessons
What design is for; principles (contrast, repetition, alignment, proximity, hierarchy, balance, whitespace); typography (anatomy, classification, pairing, leading/kerning/measure); grids and layout; color in design; imagery; logos and identity systems; posters; editorial/book design; packaging; web and UI basics; accessibility; vector vs raster; print production (bleed, CMYK, resolution); design-history touchpoints (Swiss style; Bauhaus mention only, there is a separate design-movements course); critique; process (brief to delivery); ethics; portfolio and working as a designer.

## audio-engineering — "Audio Engineering", accent #00DAF3, 26 lessons
Sound and hearing, decibels, frequency, waveform; digital audio (sample rate, bit depth, Nyquist, dither); microphones (types, patterns, placement); signal flow; gain staging; monitoring and room acoustics; recording sessions; editing; mixing (balance, EQ, compression, reverb/delay, panning, automation, buses); loudness (LUFS framed carefully); mastering overview; stereo/surround/spatial overview; live sound basics; audio for film/post and Foley; sound design; noise and grounding; file formats (lossless vs lossy); career and ethics. Every calculation (dB, Nyquist, bit rate) must be exactly correct.

## film-directing — "Film Directing", accent #E23B6D, 24 lessons (a partial broken file exists; OVERWRITE it completely)
What a director does; reading a script; vision and tone; working with actors (casting, rehearsal, blocking, direction); the camera (shot sizes, angles, movement, lenses at overview, coverage, the 180-degree rule); shot lists and storyboards; visual language and mise-en-scene; editing awareness (continuity, montage); sound and music; working with DP/designer/editor; scheduling and shooting days; set leadership and safety; documentary/animation/short vs feature; directing approaches described accurately (Hitchcock, Kurosawa, Ozu, Varda, Spike Lee, Kubrick, Ava DuVernay: only well-established facts); notes and post; festivals and career.

## film-producing — "Film Producing", accent #C9871F, 22 lessons
Types of producers; development (finding material, options, rights chain); packaging; pitching; budgets (above/below the line, top sheet, contingency); schedules (breakdown, stripboard); crew hiring and unions at overview; insurance; production workflow; post supervision; delivery; marketing and festival strategy; sales and distribution at overview; independent vs studio; documentary producing; streaming at overview; risk management; ethics and safety; producer-director relationship. Do not duplicate the existing `film-business` course in depth. Dollar figures illustrative.

## art-movements-classical — "Art Movements I: Ancient to Impressionism", accent #C9871F, 28 lessons
One movement/period per 1-2 lessons: prehistoric and ancient (Egypt, Mesopotamia, Greece, Rome), Byzantine, Islamic art and calligraphy, Romanesque and Gothic, Chinese and Japanese traditions (Song landscape, ukiyo-e), Indigenous and African traditions at overview (careful, attributed), Early and High Renaissance, Northern Renaissance, Mannerism, Baroque, Dutch Golden Age, Rococo, Neoclassicism, Romanticism, Realism, Pre-Raphaelites, Impressionism, Japonisme. Context, characteristics, key works/artists (certain facts only), techniques, how to look, what it reacted against. `art-masters` covers individual masters; do not duplicate biographies.

## art-movements-modern — "Art Movements II: Post-Impressionism to Today", accent #D40055, 28 lessons
Post-Impressionism, Symbolism, Fauvism, Expressionism (Die Brucke, Der Blaue Reiter), Cubism, Futurism, Dada, Surrealism, Constructivism and De Stijl, Suprematism, Harlem Renaissance, Mexican muralism, Abstract Expressionism, Pop Art, Minimalism, Conceptual art, Op and Kinetic art, Land art, Performance art, Feminist art, Photorealism, Neo-Expressionism, Postmodernism, street art and graffiti, digital and new-media art, contemporary global art, art and markets. Do NOT include Art Deco or Bauhaus (separate design-movements course).

## music-genres — "Music Genres: Deep Dives", accent #FFD24A, 28 lessons
One genre/family per 1-2 lessons: origins, defining sound, instruments/technology, key figures and landmark records (only well-established facts), context, how to listen, descendants: blues, gospel and spirituals, jazz, country and bluegrass, R&B and soul, rock and roll, funk, reggae and dub, hip-hop (elements, eras, production), punk and new wave, metal, disco and house, techno and electronic dance, ambient, pop eras, Latin traditions (salsa, bossa nova, tango, cumbia, reggaeton), Afrobeat/Afropop, K-pop at overview, folk traditions. `music-history-eras` and `music-figures` cover chronology and people; do not duplicate. No copyrighted lyrics.

## lit-studies-modern — "Literature Deep Study II: 1800 to Today", accent #D40055, 28 lessons
Close-reading and critical thinking: Romanticism (Wordsworth, Keats, Shelley, Mary Shelley); Victorian novel (Austen, Bronte sisters, Dickens, Eliot); American Romanticism/Renaissance (Hawthorne, Melville, Whitman, Dickinson, Poe); Russian realism; French realism and symbolism; slave narratives and African American literature (Douglass, Du Bois, Harlem Renaissance); Modernism (Woolf, Joyce, Eliot, Kafka); Lost Generation; Southern Gothic; magical realism (Garcia Marquez, Borges); postcolonial literature at overview; Indigenous literatures of North America; drama (Ibsen, Chekhov, Williams, Hansberry); poetry movements; science fiction and fantasy; graphic narrative overview; contemporary global fiction; critical lenses. Only dates you are certain of; quote nothing copyrighted and nothing over 15 words. `classic-literature` has book-by-book guides; do not duplicate.

## history-north-america — "History of North America", accent #1D4ED8, 32 lessons, 6-7 tracks
Indigenous civilisations before 1492; contact and colonisation (Spanish, French, English, Dutch); Atlantic slave trade and slavery; colonial societies; Revolution and founding; early republic; expansion and Indigenous removal; Texas/Mexican-American War; antebellum reform; Civil War; Reconstruction; Gilded Age; immigration; Progressive Era; WWI; 1920s; Depression and New Deal; WWII; Cold War; civil rights; 1960s-70s; Canada (confederation, First Nations/Metis/Inuit); Mexico (independence to revolution); late 20th century to today. Multi-perspective, causes and consequences. `founding-documents` covers primary documents; do not duplicate.

## history-south-america — "History of South America and Latin America", accent #06D6A0, 28 lessons
Andean and Amazonian peoples before contact (Caral, Chavin, Moche, Nazca, Tiwanaku, Inca, Tupi-Guarani, Mapuche); Spanish and Portuguese conquests and epidemics; colonial economy (mining, haciendas, sugar, encomienda, mita); slavery and Afro-Latin American history; Brazil as a colony; race and caste categories; Bourbon reforms; independence (Bolivar, San Martin, Haiti's influence, Brazil's route); nineteenth-century states and caudillos; export economies; abolition; immigration; War of the Pacific and Paraguayan War; twentieth-century populism; Mexican and Cuban revolutions as context; dictatorships and the Cold War; debt crises; democratisation; Indigenous movements; contemporary challenges. Hedge contested numbers.

## history-europe — "History of Europe", accent #3B82F6, 32 lessons, 6-7 tracks
Bronze Age and Greece; Rome (Republic to fall); late antiquity and Byzantium; early medieval kingdoms; Islamic Iberia; Vikings; feudalism and the Church; Crusades; Black Death; late medieval state; Renaissance and printing; Reformation and religious wars; exploration and empire; absolutism and Enlightenment; scientific revolution; French Revolution and Napoleon; Industrial Revolution; nationalism and 1848; unification of Italy and Germany; imperialism; WWI; Russian revolutions; interwar Europe and fascism; WWII and the Holocaust; Cold War Europe; decolonisation; European integration; 1989 and after. Describe atrocities plainly and accurately; hedge contested numbers.

## history-asia — "History of Asia", accent #E23B6D, 32 lessons, 6-7 tracks
East, South, Southeast and Central Asia: Indus Valley; early China (Shang, Zhou, Qin, Han, Silk Road); Indian empires (Maurya, Gupta); Buddhism and Hinduism's spread (descriptive); Tang and Song; Mongol Empire; Ming and Qing; Japan (Heian, Kamakura, Tokugawa, Meiji); Korea; Southeast Asia (Khmer, Srivijaya, Vietnam, Siam, Majapahit); Mughal India; European trade and colonialism; Opium Wars; Taiping; British Raj, independence and Partition; imperial Japan and WWII in Asia; Chinese revolutions and PRC; Korean War; Vietnam wars; decolonisation; Asian economic rise; contemporary Asia. Not the Middle East (separate course).

## history-arab-world — "History of the Arab World and the Middle East", accent #C9871F, 28 lessons
Mesopotamia, Egypt and the Levant as background; pre-Islamic Arabia; rise of Islam and early caliphates (descriptive, attributing to sources, respectful of Muslim understanding); Umayyads and Abbasids; Baghdad and the House of Wisdom; science, philosophy and literature of the Islamic Golden Age; Fatimids; Andalus; Crusades from multiple sides; Mongol invasions; Mamluks; Ottoman Empire; Safavids at overview; Arab cultural life (poetry, architecture, music, calligraphy); European imperialism and mandates; Arab nationalism and the Arab Revolt; creation of modern states; the Palestine/Israel question (even-handed, factual, contested narratives named as such); Suez Crisis; oil; Iranian Revolution as context; wars in Iraq and Lebanon; Arab Spring; contemporary issues.

## world-mythology — "Myths and Mythology of the World", accent #7A2BD6, 32 lessons, 7 tracks
How to study myth (comparative, functional, structural, historical); Mesopotamia (Enuma Elish, Gilgamesh); Egypt; Greece and Rome; Norse and Germanic (via the Eddas, caveats); Celtic (via medieval Irish and Welsh sources, caveats); Slavic at overview with caveats; Hindu epics and Vedic myth; Buddhist jataka; China (Pangu, Journey to the West); Japan (Kojiki, Nihon Shoki); Korea at overview; Polynesian and Maori; Aboriginal Australian Dreaming (respectful; stories belong to specific nations, some restricted); African traditions (Yoruba, Akan, Dogon at overview, caveats); Mesoamerican (Popol Vuh, Aztec); Andean; North American Indigenous traditions (attributed to specific nations); Persian (Shahnameh) and Arabian; Biblical and Quranic narratives as part of world storytelling (respectful, descriptive); recurring motifs compared (floods, tricksters); heroes' journeys and critiques; myth in modern media. Treat living religions with respect; name surviving sources.
